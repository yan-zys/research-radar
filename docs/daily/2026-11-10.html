推荐理由：该论文为猪尾猕猴肺部单细胞图谱，属于非神经/非脑组织的临床前模型研究，虽为灵长类单细胞图谱但缺乏演化比较视角，与研究者聚焦脑/神经系统演化、跨物种细胞类型同源性比较的核心方向不符。
背景：肺部在呼吸系统疾病中经历显著的生理和免疫变化，需要上皮、内皮、基质和免疫细胞的协同响应。非人灵长类因其解剖、生理和免疫特征与人类高度相似，是研究肺部生物学和呼吸道疾病的重要模型。然而，猪尾猕猴肺部的细胞多样性和分子特征尚未被系统阐明，限制了其在疾病机制、疫苗和药物研发中的精准应用。
研究方法：研究者取5只成年雌性猪尾猕猴（Macaca nemestrina）的肺实质组织，消化为单细胞悬液后采用10x Genomics 3'单细胞RNA测序。通过去除环境RNA和质量控制后，使用scVI和scANVI进行数据整合。基于无监督聚类和子聚类，结合经典标志基因进行人工注释。最后利用scArches将数据映射至人类肺细胞图谱（HLCA），并通过Seurat标签转移与恒河猴免疫参考图谱（RIRA）进行比对。
研究结果：该图谱共包含61,323个细胞，涵盖上皮、间充质、内皮、淋巴和髓系等37种细胞类型，包括肺泡I型和II型细胞、棒状和多纤毛细胞、气细胞毛细血管、淋巴管内皮细胞、终末前细支气管分泌细胞，以及CD4+和CD8+ T细胞、γδ T细胞、MAIT样细胞、固有样CD8+ T细胞和第3组固有淋巴细胞等。与人HLCA Level 4注释相比，AT1和AT2细胞一致率分别为96%和93%，B细胞为94-97%，CD4+ T细胞为99-100%，经典和非经典单核细胞分别为84%和85%，肺泡巨噬细胞为80%，肥大细胞为96%。免疫群体与RIRA的映射保真度相似（肺泡巨噬细胞和B细胞达99%）。
研究意义：该研究为猪尾猕猴肺部提供了首个全面的单细胞参考图谱，证实了其与人肺在细胞类型水平的高度相似性，为利用非人灵长类模型研究呼吸道感染、疫苗开发和药物疗效评估奠定了重要的细胞学基础。然而，该研究局限于单一器官的临床前模型构建，未涉及跨物种演化比较或神经系统相关分析，对理解器官演化或神经系统的演化创新无直接贡献。

英文摘要（原文）：Background: The lungs undergo substantial physiological and immunological changes during respiratory illness to meet the host's increased metabolic and oxygen demands. These responses require coordination among epithelial, endothelial, stromal, and immune cells throughout the respiratory zone, interstitium, and vasculature. Nonhuman primates (NHPs) are a valuable model for studying pulmonary biology as they have similar anatomic, physiologic, and immunologic correspondence to humans. Despite the widespread use of NHPs in pulmonary research, the cellular diversity and molecular characteristics of the pigtail macaque lung remain incompletely defined. Methods: Parenchymal lung tissue from five adult female pigtail macaques (Macaca nemestrina) was dissociated into single cells and profiled using 10x Genomics 3' single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq). Next, ambient RNA was removed and quality control analyses performed. Samples were then integrated using scVI and scANVI. Cell types were labeled after unsupervised clustering and subclustering, followed by manual annotation using canonical marker genes. We compared lineages and cell types with the Level 4 Human Lung Cell Atlas (HLCA) using scArches reference mapping and with the Rhesus Immune Reference Atlas (RIRA) using Seurat label transfer. Results: The atlas includes 61,323 cells representing 37 cell types in epithelial, mesenchymal, endothelial, lymphoid, and myeloid populations. Annotated populations included both major and rare lung cell types, including alveolar type I and II cells, club and multiciliated cells, aerocyte capillary, and lymphatic endothelial cells, pre-terminal bronchiole secretory cells, CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, and several innate-like lymphocyte populations (gamma delta T cells, MAIT-like cells, innate-like CD8+ T cells, and group 3 innate lymphoid cells). There was strong concordance at HLCA annotation level 4, including AT1 and AT2 cells (96% and 93%), B cells (94 to 97%), CD4+ T cells (99 to 100%), classical and non-classical monocytes (84% and 85%), alveolar macrophages (80%), and mast cells (96%). Immune populations mapped to the RIRA with similar fidelity (alveolar macrophages and B cells, 99%). Conclusions: While studies of pandemic respiratory infections, vaccines, and therapeutics rely on NHP disease models, this atlas provides a unique reference for epithelial, endothelial, stromal, and immune cell states in the pigtail macaque lung. We found that single-cell populations from the pigtail macaque lung are highly similar to those in the human lung at the lineage level. This reference will enable studies of pathogenesis and vaccine and drug efficacy in a highly translational model of the human lung.
