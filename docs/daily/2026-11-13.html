英文摘要（原文）：Neuronal dendrites form highly branched morphologies supported by spatially patterned microtubule arrays that are essential for intracellular transport and structural stability, yet how this organization is established and maintained remains unclear. Here we analyze microtubule behavior in Drosophila sensory neurons using live-cell imaging, deep learning-based analysis, and genetic perturbations. We find that microtubules are shorter and fewer in thin distal branches and progressively longer and more abundant in thicker proximal branches. Locally, polymerization slows in narrow branches due to soluble tubulin depletion, while catastrophe frequency increases near branch points upon cortical encounter. A physical model incorporating these morphology-dependent constraints reproduces both local microtubule dynamics and global organization. Inverting the textbook view that microtubule dynamics shape cell morphology, our results demonstrate that, on short timescales, cell morphology instead constrains microtubule dynamics, thereby determining large-scale cytoskeletal organization.

英文摘要（原文）：The blood-brain barrier (BBB) regulates the entry of chemicals into the central nervous system, and contaminants with high permeability can accumulate in the brain, posing neurotoxicity risks. Given the urgent need for high-throughput neurotoxicity assessment, this study proposes a multifeature fusion framework for identifying and quantifying the BBB permeability to neurotoxic chemicals. BBBProfiler leverages an SE(3)-Transformer to encode the three-dimensional molecular geometry and a deep neural network to encode collision cross-section (CCS) and fingerprint, followed by adaptive integration of modality representations through gating weights. Model predictions were supplemented with natural-language explanations generated from a large language model. BBBProfiler is deployed on a publicly accessible platform (https://www.ai4environ.cn/BBBProfiler) to facilitate the development of new approach methodologies for neurotoxicity evaluation.

Recent deep learning techniques, particularly graph neural networks (GNNs), have shown strong potential in modeling SC-FC coupling relationships. To address these issues, we propose a Causality-Aware Graph Coupling Network (CA-GCN) that disentangles causal representations and models causal SC-FC coupling patterns in a task-relevant manner. Notably, VNN outcomes are derived by filtering inputs along covariance eigenvectors, providing a mechanism for explaining their decisions in terms of latent (orthogonal) vectors. 英文摘要（原文）：Predicting the bioactivity profiles of small molecules against G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) is a challenge in drug discovery. Although deep learning has accelerated the prediction of binding affinities, existing approaches often struggle to distinguish between functional efficacies because they neglect dynamic conformational equilibria. Furthermore, structure-based methods are frequently limited by the scarcity of high-resolution active-state crystal structures and the indistinguishability of conformational states in static representations. To bridge the gap between black-box prediction and biophysical reality, we propose Dual-State Query (DSQ), a physics-informed multimodal architecture that explicitly embeds the Monod-Wyman-Changeux (MWC) model of allostery within a neural network. Unlike conventional models that rely on explicit 3D structures, DSQ utilizes learnable orthogonal queries to extract disentangled representations of active and inactive receptor states. These latent representations are governed by a novel neural MWC gating module, which mathematically derives the probability of receptor activation from thermodynamic competition between ligand-state affinities and the receptor's intrinsic conformational energy barrier. A contrastive ranking objective is also introduced to enforce differential affinity constraints, ensuring physical consistency. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DSQ outperforms other baselines, particularly for the agonist subset. Additional homology-stratified, temperature-sensitivity, perturbation, clustering, and efficiency analyses show that DSQ provides useful thermodynamic inductive bias, while also exposing a clear limitation on low-homology receptors. The code is available at https://github.com/jiankliu/DSQ.

By creating a shared workspace for pathologists and bioinformaticians, it operationalizes an expert-in-the-loop approach and makes feature-based multimodal registration accessible without specialized computer-vision expertise or high-performance computing.

英文摘要（原文）：Developmental variation has often been appreciated as a major phenotypic substrate of evolutionary innovation in multicellular organisms. Although quantification of developmental variation in isogenic populations has a long history, it has been difficult to accomplish across natural populations until recently. Given its invariant cell lineage, Caenorhabditis elegans represents an ideal model system for studying how natural diversity in development is expressed at the resolution of single cells, cell lineages, and the whole embryo. Using embGAN, a deep-learning pipeline that enables label-free lineage tracing without the bottleneck and potential confounding effects of generating transgenic strains, we reconstructed the early embryonic lineage of 78 embryos spanning eight genetic backgrounds, including six wild strains, and quantified cell-cycle timing with a lineage-aligned graph metric that separates global rescaling of developmental rate from localized changes in lineage structure. We found that cell-cycle timing differs reproducibly among genotypes, with between-strain variation consistently exceeding within-strain variation. The magnitude of this variation was itself genotype-specific and not correlated with levels of genetic divergence. Unlike the global shifts in the tempo of development that dominate divergence between species, these differences are predominantly in lineage patterning, changing the timing of specific divisions, and they align to cell fate. Thus, the invariant lineage of C. elegans is built on a structured, genotype-specific landscape of developmental variation, a potential source of trait variation from which drift in developmental systems can emerge.

英文摘要（原文）：Spatial transcriptomics (ST) has become a central tool in tissue biology, with multiple technologies addressing the issue from a different angle. Each platform imposes its own raw data format conventions, and few workflows address reproducible, scalable, cohort-level analyses while integrating expert-supervised decisions to strengthen computational and biological provenance. In this work, we present SpaceBlocks, a Snakemake workflow for reproducible cohort-level analysis of single-cell resolution-ST data that prioritizes expert supervision over full automation. SpaceBlocks homogenizes heterogeneous platform outputs into a common data format, whose structure is validated to ensure downstream processing. Comprehensive expert supervision is facilitated through per sample quality control and cell type annotation diagnostic visualizations, after which downstream analyses automatically follow. SpaceBlocks also allows the ingestion of technology-agnostic externally formatted data and provides interoperability with existing tools and technologies. We demonstrate the use of SpaceBlocks through the reanalysis of 37 samples from public Visium HD, Xenium 5K and MERSCOPE datasets. SpaceBlocks exhibited processing scalability across the three platforms. This reanalysis identified previously missed cell types in Visium HD and heterogeneous marker gene expression in MERSCOPE, highlighting the need for expert-supervised annotations. Moreover, SpaceBlocks facilitated the identification of differentially expressed genes associated with previously defined multiple sclerosis (MS) spatial domains in MERSCOPE, and transcriptional differences between healthy and malignant colorectal tissues in Visium HD. Together, these findings establish SpaceBlocks as a flexible and reproducible workflow for expert-supervised spatial transcriptomics analysis across technologies and biological cohorts.
