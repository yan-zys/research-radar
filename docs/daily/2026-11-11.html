推荐理由：该论文直接涉及神经和感觉分泌细胞类型的起源与演化，运用SAMap进行跨物种单细胞图谱比较，与研究者关注的核心问题高度一致。
背景：特化细胞类型的起源与多样化是重构动物演化的核心问题。神经和感觉分泌细胞类型的演化起源尤为引人关注，而早期分化的非神经元动物如海绵为理解前神经分子工具包提供了关键线索。自由游动的海绵和刺胞动物幼体阶段为研究这些问题提供了独特背景，因为这些幼体依赖特化的感觉和信号系统来检测环境线索并协调散布和附着行为。
研究方法：研究者整合海绵Amphimedon queenslandica和四种刺胞动物（Nematostella vectensis、Astroides calycularis、Pocillopora acuta和Clytia hemisphaerica）的幼体单细胞转录组数据集。采用SAMap（Self-Assembling Manifold mapping）进行跨物种细胞类型映射和比较分析，进行种内和种间转录组整合与相似性评估，并分析神经相关基因模块在不同细胞类型中的分布模式。
研究结果：SAMap分析显示刺胞动物间主要细胞类型身份（包括神经、分泌、刺细胞、上皮、胃皮层和顶端细胞群体）具有广泛保守性。将比较扩展至海绵后，发现多种海绵幼体细胞群体与刺胞动物的神经、感觉和祖细胞相关细胞类型存在转录相似性。这些细胞群体在海绵幼体内仍与更广泛的上皮和感觉细胞类型保持转录整合。神经相关基因模块，包括与光感受、神经命运决定和突触后支架相关的保守家族，分布于不同的上皮和感觉样海绵幼体细胞群体中，而非局限于单一原始神经细胞类型。
研究意义：该研究支持分子组件随后整合入神经系统的组分在海绵和刺胞动物共同祖先中已广泛分布于多种细胞类型的观点。研究结果为神经和感觉分泌细胞类型通过多细胞类型祖先分子程序的多样化和重组而产生的演化情景提供了证据，对理解神经系统演化中细胞类型同源性、独立起源假说与共同起源假说之间的辩论具有重要参考价值。
中文全摘要：神经和感觉分泌细胞类型的起源是动物演化中的关键问题。研究者整合海绵Amphimedon queenslandica和四种刺胞动物（Nematostella vectensis、Astroides calycularis、Pocillopora acuta和Clytia hemisphaerica）的幼体单细胞转录组数据，通过SAMap跨物种映射发现刺胞动物主要细胞类型间的广泛保守性，并揭示海绵幼体细胞群体与刺胞动物神经、感觉及祖细胞类型的转录相似性。神经相关基因模块在海绵中分布于多种上皮和感觉样细胞中，支持神经和感觉分泌细胞类型起源于祖先分子程序重组的演化模型。
英文摘要（原文）：Understanding how specialised cell types emerged and diversified is fundamental to reconstructing animal evolution. The origins of neural and sensory-secretory cell types are particularly intriguing, and early diverging non-neuronal metazoans, such as sponges, provide critical insights into pre-neural molecular toolkits. Free-swimming larval stages of sponges and cnidarians provide an unique context for investigating these questions, as these larvae rely on specialised sensory and signalling systems to detect environmental cues and coordinate behaviour during dispersal and settlement. Here, we integrated larval single-cell transcriptomic datasets from the demosponge Amphimedon queenslandica and four cnidarian species (Nematostella vectensis, Astroides calycularis, Pocillopora acuta and Clytia hemisphaerica). Cross-species mapping using Self-Assembling Manifold mapping (SAMap) recovered broad conservation of major cell-type identities among cnidarians, including neural, secretory, cnidocyte, epithelial, gastrodermal and apical populations. Extending these comparisons to A. queenslandica displayed transcriptional similarities between several sponge larval cell populations and cnidarian neural, sensory and progenitor-associated cell types. Within-species analyses showed that these cell populations remained transcriptionally integrated within the broader epithelial and sensory cell type of the sponge larva. Neural-associated gene modules, including conserved families associated with photoreception, neural fate specification and postsynaptic scaffolding, were distributed across distinct epithelial and sensory-like sponge larval cell populations rather than being restricted to a single proto-neural cell type. Our findings suggest that molecular components subsequently incorporated into neural systems were already deployed across diverse cell types in a common ancestor of sponges & cnidarians. Our work supports an evolutionary scenario in which extant neural and sensory-secretory cell types arose through the diversification and reorganisation of molecular programmes distributed across multiple ancestral sensory-secretory cell types.

英文摘要（原文）：Understanding how human organogenesis is accomplished via intricate spatiotemporal patterning of organ primordia requires spatial atlases at cellular resolution. However, existing human embryo spatial atlases are built using sequencing-based platforms such as Stereo-seq, which have limited spatial resolution and blurred anatomical boundaries, leading to misclassification across adjacent cells. Here, we integrate in situ imaging-based transcriptomics (Xenium) with transcriptome-wide spatial profiling (Visium-HD) of Carnegie stages 9-12 embryos to delineate the patterning of core embryonic tissues at single-cell resolution, including the brain, spinal cord, somites, neural crest, notochord, heart, and gut tube. A new finding enabled by spatial single-cell resolution is the identification of rostral midline progenitors (RMPs), a cell population co-expressing SOX2, TBXT, and OTX2. Transcriptionally, RMPs are distinct from both neuromesodermal progenitors (NMPs) and prechordal mesoderm, and in vivo clonal barcoding in mice indicates that RMPs share a common developmental origin with rostral neural ectoderm. To interrogate their functional significance, we leveraged our spatial transcriptome data to recapitulate RMP-like cells from human pluripotent stem cells and showed that these cells can induce epiblast-like cells to acquire mesendodermal and endodermal fates, while themselves adopting an ectodermal fate consistent with their in vivo counterparts. This work provides a 3D, stage-resolved molecular cell atlas of tissue patterning in human embryo that serves as a reference for in vitro models.

英文摘要（原文）：Inter-individual variability in phenotypic traits is widespread in nature both across and within species, with similar patterns of variation often occurring between and within species. Given that these two types of variation are generated by evolutionary and plasticity processes respectively, it raises the question to what extent the causal mechanisms underlying such variability at these two different levels are shared or not. To address this question, we studied the variation in brain transcriptomes in Tanganyikan cichlids that exhibit both intra- and interspecific variation in social behavior. We compared two facultatively social cichlid species, each containing naturally occurring solitary and social-living individuals, with a closely related obligatory solitary resembling the ancestral state. Differential gene expression and Gene Set Enrichment Analyses were performed in the telencephalon and diencephalon transcriptomes to investigate the molecular basis of both behavioral plasticity and the evolution of sociality. Behavioral plasticity involved few differentially expressed genes but extensive changes in biological pathways, supporting a highly polygenic architecture of social behavior. Across both species, social-living individuals consistently upregulated pathways involved in translation, protein homeostasis, and energy metabolism, whereas solitary individuals showed enrichment of neuronal morphogenesis, cell adhesion, and structural plasticity. Remarkably, these same functional programs were associated with the independent evolution of sociality, revealing a strong overlap between evolutionary and plastic transcriptional responses. This convergence was largely restricted to the telencephalon, while the diencephalon showed limited and inconsistent functional signatures. Together, our results support the repeated recruitment of gene regulatory programs during the evolution of sociality and demonstrate that behavioral plasticity and evolutionary transitions share common neurogenomic responses, consistent with a conserved genetic toolkit for social behavior.
